The actual science

What "detecting manganese from space" really means

No satellite carries a sensor that reads out "manganese: yes/no." What Landsat and Sentinel-2 actually measure is reflected sunlight, in a handful of wavelength bands, off the top few millimetres of the ground. Manganese and iron oxides absorb and reflect that light in a distinctive pattern — darker in the blue, brighter in the red, with specific SWIR behaviour where hydroxyl-bearing alteration minerals sit alongside them.

Band ratios exploit that pattern. Dividing one band by another cancels out illumination and topographic shading effects that would otherwise swamp the mineral signal, leaving a number that tracks composition instead of just "how sunny was it that day." That's the entire trick — and it's forty-year-old remote sensing science (Sabins, 1999), not something new to this project.

What this can and can't tell you

It can flag surface pixels whose spectral signature resembles known manganese-associated alteration. It cannot see through soil, vegetation or rock cover, and it says nothing about depth, grade, or tonnage. Every output is a screening layer for field geologists — never a substitute for them.

MORI Manganese Oxide Ratio Index
Red / Blue

Manganese and iron oxides absorb blue light strongly and reflect more in red — this ratio pulls that contrast forward.

HAI Hydroxyl Alteration Index
SWIR1 / SWIR2

Flags clay and hydroxyl-bearing alteration zones — the weathering signature that often sits alongside supergene-enriched ore.

FMI Ferrous Mineral Index
SWIR1 / NIR

Picks out Fe²⁺ signatures in the mafic host rocks tied to gondite and BIF-hosted manganese sequences.

NDVI Vegetation Index — used as a mask
(NIR−Red) / (NIR+Red)

Not a mineral signal at all. Thick vegetation hides the ground entirely, so NDVI is used to down-weight or exclude covered pixels rather than to find ore.

  • Real CHIRPS rainfall via Earth Engine
  • Real MOIL / IBM Yearbook production history

Beyond the 12-hour build

Where this goes next

Near-real-time refresh

Scheduled Earth Engine exports on a cron trigger, so the prospectivity layer updates automatically as new cloud-free scenes become available — genuinely continuous, without pretending any sensor detects minerals instantaneously.

PRISMA hyperspectral integration

239 narrow bands instead of 6-7 broad ones, once registration clears — sharper separation between manganese-oxide and look-alike iron-oxide signatures.

Spatial-CV-backed confidence intervals

Report an uncertainty band on every prospectivity score, not just a point estimate — so field teams know which targets are worth the fuel to reach first.

Expand beyond the MOIL footprint

Extend the same pipeline to other GSI-mapped manganese belts nationally, feeding directly into the National Critical Mineral Mission's exploration push.
